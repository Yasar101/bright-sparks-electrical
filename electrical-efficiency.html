Behind-the-Meter Inefficiencies

Why commercial power distribution silently wastes capital.

Commercial facilities with inductive loads (motors, chillers, pumps, transformers) draw substantial reactive power that generates heat, causes voltage drops, and increases kVA capacity charges.

Common Electrical Distribution Losses

  • Reactive power penalties: Utility supplier line-item fines for low power factor (< 0.95 lag)
  • Severe phase imbalance: Single-phase loads unevenly loaded across 3 phases, causing high neutral currents and nuisance breaker tripping
  • Harmonic distortion: High total harmonic distortion (THDi) from VFDs and IT power supplies damaging sensitive electronics
  • Aging switchgear: Obsolete fuse boards with degraded contact resistance and lack of surge protection
  • Thermal hotspots: Loose busbar joints and overloaded terminals posing severe arc-fault and fire hazards

The Bright Sparks Engineered Solution

  • Automatic Power Factor Correction (PFC): Detuned capacitor banks step in dynamically to maintain power factor above 0.98
  • Phase load rebalancing: Methodical redistributing of single-phase sub-circuits across all 3 phases to reduce neutral return current
  • Harmonic filtering: Passive and active harmonic filters to protect electronic drives and reduce cable overheating
  • Switchgear modernisation: Modern 3-phase panel boards with Type 1+2 surge protection devices (SPDs) compliant with BS 7671
  • Thermographic testing & EICRs: Calibrated FLIR infrared surveys and complete 5-year periodic testing for compliance and insurance

Electrical FAQs

Electrical efficiency & compliance questions.

Guidance for duty holders, operations directors, and facilities managers.

How frequently does a commercial facility require an EICR?

Under British Standard BS 7671 and the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989, commercial buildings typically require an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) every 5 years (or 3 years for industrial facilities with harsh environments). In addition, commercial property insurers almost universally mandate a valid, satisfactory EICR as a condition of fire insurance coverage.

How can we tell if our facility needs Power Factor Correction?

Look closely at your half-hourly electricity bill. If you see charges labelled "Reactive Power Charges", "Exceeded Capacity", or if your power factor is listed below 0.95, a PFC system will immediately cut your costs. You can email us a copy of your recent utility bill and our engineers will calculate your potential savings free of charge.

What is the difference between active power (kW) and reactive power (kVAR)?

Active power (kW) is the real power that performs useful work (lighting lamps, turning shafts, generating heat). Reactive power (kVAR) is the power required to establish magnetic fields in inductive devices like motors and transformers. While reactive power does no productive work, it must still be supplied by the grid, drawing extra current and overloading cables unless corrected on-site with capacitors.

Can electrical testing be conducted out-of-hours?

Yes. To minimise disruption to critical operations, IT servers, and production lines, our commercial electrical inspection and testing teams regularly work during evening shifts, weekends, or scheduled factory shutdowns.
